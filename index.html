YOUR FLEET — THREE AIRCRAFT, THREE JOBS

MAVIC 3E · endurance. The workhorse with the longest battery — 43-minute flights in the real fleet. First pick for long routes and big surveys.

MAVIC 2T · thermal. The only aircraft with a thermal camera; when the rangers call, they mean this one. Shorter battery.

MINI 4 PRO · speed. The sprinter — 34 minutes, 249 grams in the real fleet. Fastest across the conservancy, perfect courier; drains quickest.

Assign an aircraft to a request and the pre-flight gate decides. When it refuses, the card names the rule that fired and the fix. Nothing flies on charm.

Aircraft and specs mirror the real Ol Pejeta campaign fleet (Maalouf et al., ICUAS 2025). And like the real campaign: we fly west of the river.

AIRCRAFT LOST

The battery ran out before the drone made it home. Recovery team dispatched; the accountant has been notified. It happens to every coordinator once.

SHIFT DEBRIEF — 17:00

MORNING ASSIGNMENT

Good morning, coordinator. Three rules before the rotors turn:

  1. Photograph the giraffe herd and the zebra herd. Fly into the green ring and hold steady; the camera fires itself.
  2. Never cross a red circle. Drone noise disturbs the animals; the closer you fly, the worse it gets. The science is on this page, one tap away.
  3. Bring the drone home to the pad before the battery dies. You have one aircraft and the accountant knows its price.
WHY THE RED CIRCLE EXISTS

"Higher altitudes corresponded to only brief vigilance by multiple individuals… at the lowest altitudes (below 40 m), sustained vigilance and short-distance relocation were more common."

"Behavioral responses appeared to vary with flight altitude and group composition, with greater disturbance recorded during dual-drone operations and in mixed-species assemblages."

Afridi, Laporte-Devylder, Maalouf, …, Schultz Lundquist et al., "Wildlife Responses to Drone Noise: A Preliminary Approach for Quantifying Disturbance", Ecology and Evolution, 2026. Field site: Ol Pejeta Conservancy — the same zebras you are photographing.

LEVEL 1 COMPLETE — 09:00

The clipboard is yours now, coordinator. Level 2: the operations desk. Three aircraft, a queue of requests, and a pre-flight gate that only speaks in rules.

THE OPERATIONS DESK · requests arrive on their own schedule

Level 1: drag a line from the drone to fly it. Level 2: assign aircraft to requests and let the gate judge your plans. Sound on is worth it: OPSY, your operations assistant, has opinions.

Level 1 is the pilot's seat; Level 2 is the operations desk — finish the morning assignment and press ▶ TAKE THE DESK. Every refusal the pre-flight gate issues cites the study it comes from, and at 17:00 your shift is scored against an autonomy layer running the same deck under the same rules.
